## 1. The one thing OneMind is #1 at

**OneMind is the self-hosted OS where AI agents can only ask. The gateway, not the prompt, makes them wait for your tap.**

Others limit what an agent can touch:
- The Home Assistant MCP Server lets agents reach only the entities you expose. Locks, alarms and cameras are left out by default ([HA mcp_server](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/mcp_server/)).
- umbrelOS 2.0 has per-agent scopes.
- Google Home MCP blocks locks and alarms entirely.

Scoping decides *what* an agent may touch. OneMind adds a step: **every action an agent wants is a request; the owner approves it with a passkey on their phone; the OS carries it out; every step goes on a hash-chained record you can check.** The gateway enforces this (`core/onemind_core/actions.py`, `auth.py`, `audit.py`; contract §3, §11.2–11.3: `/mcp` has no approve tool).

The OpenClaw incident (2026-02-23) shows why. Its "wait for approval" rule lived only in the agent's context and was dropped during compaction ([AIID 1542](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/1542/), [Galileo](https://galileo.ai/blog/openclaw-sobering-lessons-from-an-agent-gone-rogue)).

Two proofs back the claim, once hardware shows them:
- **Updates are kept only once they work.** None of the 7 A/B products in `research-output/02-competitors/MATRIX.md` §4.1 does this.
- **Nothing unscanned installs, and nothing installed escapes its namespace.** The first half is contract §11.1. The second half is new work (E0S).

**Honest scope today:** the only actions are `app.install` and `app.remove` (`executors.py`). "AI touching the real world" stays a lab demo until LT-VM-18 passes at G4. Until then, if that test fails, actuation is blocked from release (20 §G4).

> **Tagline:** *Your AI asks. You decide. The box remembers.*

## What the skeptic changed

1. **Repo state was overstated.** Main is `b0d527a`. Of wave 2, only `gwmcp` has commits; `hermes` and `seeds2` are empty. E0 is now 0–2 weeks, and the Hermes and seed work moved into E2 and E5.
2. **Security hole added as a blocker (E0S).** The executor runs `kubectl apply -k` with no allowlist of resource types. As things stand, an install an agent requests and the owner approves could hand an app cluster-admin.
3. **Sequencing errors fixed.**
   - Passkeys and a PWA need HTTPS, but E3 came before E13 and E8. HTTPS and an owner passkey are now part of E3.
   - The 30-day Pi soak has to start by 12-11 to finish by G3.
4. **Gate conflict made visible.** `20-PLAN` gate content is still synced to Ubuntu; the plan said "unchanged". It now gets rewritten for D-007.
5. **Actuation tied to G4.** HA writes, `cmd.*` and the robot sim are lab-only until LT-VM-18 passes. PX4 and drone control were dropped.
6. **Licensing misread fixed.** D-001 forbids copying code. It does not forbid packaging GPL or AGPL apps, so "Apache-2.0 sources only" became "fresh manifests, licences classed L1/L2/blocked".
7. **Hype cut.**
   - "Only OS" was dropped: HA already scopes agents to exposed entities.
   - "Tamper-evident" is now backed by a checkpoint outside the box.
   - 100 apps became 30.
   - Local AI is out of the top 10.
8. **Obvious wins added:** installer mode on Debian, the agent kill switch, approve screens that show effects worked out by the gateway rather than the agent's text, and a limit on pending agent requests.
9. **Capacity made explicit:** about 300 build hours in 13 weeks, with weeks 6–7 at half capacity.
