Directive on repair of goods
Directive on repair of goods
Promoting repair contributes to sustainable consumption.
    Page contents
Page contents
          Directive (EU) 2024/1799 on common rules promoting the repair of goods was adopted on 13 June 2024. Member States had to transpose it into national rules and apply from 31 July 2026. 
The Directive aims at promoting more sustainable consumption by increasing repair both within and outside the legal guarantee.
It is one of several pieces of legislation that collectively aim at extending the lifetime of consumer products. It complements the Union’s action in the Ecodesign framework under the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation. Ecodesign measures promote product reparability by setting requirements on, amongst others, product design and availability of spare parts. This Directive also complements Directive EU/2024/825 on Empowering Consumers in the Green Transition, which aims at providing consumers with better information on durability and reparability of goods at the point of sale.
  The main elements of the Directive
Obligation to repair
Manufacturers of products (e.g., fridges or smartphones) that are subject to reparability requirements in EU law and listed in Annex II of the Directive will have to repair those products within a reasonable time and for a reasonable price. Such repairability requirements are set in the product-specific legislation, mainly implementing the Ecodesign Directive and the Regulation on Ecodesign for Sustainable Products mentioned before.
In addition, manufacturers are prohibited from using contractual clauses, hardware or software techniques that impede the repair of goods listed in Annex II unless justified by legitimate and objective factors. Manufacturers that provide spare parts for these goods must do so at reasonable prices.
Manufacturers are also required to make available information on their repair services to consumers in an easily accessible manner. This could be done, for example, on their website or in the instruction manuals. In addition, they must inform consumers on a free access website about the indicative prices charged for typical repairs. These obligations complement product-specific legislation that requires manufacturers to provide information on websites about the spare parts and their indicative prices for specific products. 
Products subject to the obligation to repair
Annex II to the Directive provides a list of products to which the obligation to repair applies. The Commission updates this list when relevant repairability requirements are adopted for new categories of consumer goods:
domestic local space heaters were added to Annex II as from 31 July 2026, in accordance with the Commission Delegated Directive (EU) 2026/74 of 12 January 2026.
European Online Platform for repair
Consumers will be able to find repairers more easily through a new online European Repair Platform, which will be established as an extension of “Your Europe” portal. While the Commission will be responsible for setting up the IT infrastructure for this platform, the Member States will set the conditions and manage the registration of repairers established on their territory. The platform is foreseen to become operational in January 2028.
Extension of the legal guarantee after repair
The Directive also amended the Sale of goods Directive (EU) 2019/771. The amendment provides consumers with an extra year of the legal guarantee if they choose to repair the product instead of replacing it under the legal guarantee. 
National measures promoting repair
Member States must take at least one measure promoting repair on their territory. This can include both financial and non-financial measures such as information campaigns, repair vouchers, or training in repair skills.
European Repair Information Form
Repairers can inform consumers about their repair services via a standard European Repair Information Form (provided in Annex I to the Directive), which would allow consumers to compare different repair offers more easily. When the repairer chooses to provide the Form, the repair conditions set out therein must remain valid for 30 days.
  What are the next steps
The Commission continues working with the Member States on the development of the European online platform. It will also continue updating the list of products in Annex II that are subject to the obligation to repair, when repairability requirements are adopted for new categories of consumer goods in the Ecodesign framework.
