# W4 — Refuter report: P0-1 (k3s on every node with at least 4 GB, NATS leaf below that), technical lens

- **Claim under test (P0-1):** Kubernetes (k3s) should remain the app platform on every OneMind node with at least 4 GB of RAM. Smaller devices (sensors, drones, MCU robots, Pi Zero) join as NATS leaf nodes, not Kubernetes nodes. In a 4 GiB arm64 Debian 13 VM, k3s v1.37.2+k3s1 (SQLite) running a two-container app left 2,557 MiB available and wrote about 2.75 GB/day. That is about 0.95 GiB more RAM and 2.7 GB/day more writes than Podman running the same app. The only Kubernetes-based competitor (Olares) requires 8 GB; HAOS and umbrelOS use Docker.
- **Refuter lens:** technical. I was given only the claim and its cited sources (RESEARCH-MISSION.md §2 rule 7). I did not read the author's reasoning. I opened the lab's raw `RESULT.md`/`README.md` only to check the cited log.
- **Date:** 2026-11-10
- **Requirement IDs touched:** N-9, N-2, F-14, M-6, M-4, F-11

## Verdict: **weakened**

The direction holds: Kubernetes for platform nodes, NATS leaf for small devices. No source I found shows k3s failing outright on a 4 GB node with SSD storage. But the claim gates on the wrong variable and leaves out a constraint-matched counter-example:

1. **The gate is RAM, but the documented failure mode is storage.** The claim's own cited k3s page (L139-L141) says SD cards and eMMC "cannot handle the IO load". The k3s maintainer says the project does not test on NAND flash (N1). Under the claim as written, a Pi 5 4 GB booting from SD or eMMC is a k3s node, and that is the setup k3s itself warns against.
2. **"The only Kubernetes-based competitor is Olares" is incomplete in a way that matters.** TrueNAS SCALE shipped its app catalog on k3s, then **removed it and moved to Docker/Compose in 24.10** (N3, N4). It is a single-box appliance with a one-click app catalog, which is a closer constraint match to OneMind's Lite/Edge boxes than Olares is. Rancher's own k3s appliance OS, k3OS, was abandoned and archived (N5).
3. **The lab numbers are thin evidence.** One run, a 10-minute write window (18 MiB in total), a toy app (nginx + redis), virtio disk on an Apple Silicon host, and no OneMind core services. The PASS is real, but it supports "plausible on 4 GB with SSD", not "decided".

## New sources (found by searching for failure, abandonment, lifecycle cuts)

- **N1. k3s Discussion #7694, "K3s read/writes and wear on flash storage such as NAND"** — https://github.com/k3s-io/k3s/discussions/7694 (opened). The reporter measured about 3.3 GiB/day of k3s writes on an aarch64 node with 11 pods. Maintainer brandond (2022-02-10) said k3s does not target, "nor do we test on, systems that use NAND flash storage or flash-specific filesystems". The lowest target is "Raspberry Pi 3 class hardware", and he would "probably still recommend using a Pi4 or equivalent and booting off an external SSD". He also said (2022-02-07) that he did not see how to reduce writes further while keeping the datastore consistent. **The upstream project does not validate the medium most Pi 4 GB boxes ship with.**
- **N2. k3s Discussion #10306, "High CPU and disk read/write, very large (2GB) state.db"** — https://github.com/k3s-io/k3s/discussions/10306 (opened). A fleet of **200+ single-node k3s SQLite installs at customer sites**, the exact OneMind deployment shape. Affected nodes had a state.db above 2 GB (healthy under 50 MB), "Compact failed" errors and about 80% CPU. The maintainer blamed "a combination of slow disk IO and high datastore write volume". A second user (2025-05-21) reported that the fix "worked but only for a few weeks" and that the root cause was unknown. **The single-node SQLite path has a known fleet failure mode that comes back over time, and a solo founder would have to handle it remotely across customer boxes.**
- **N3. TrueNAS SCALE 24.10 "Electric Eel" release notes** — https://www.truenas.com/docs/scale/24.10/gettingstarted/scalereleasenotes/ (opened). "The TrueNAS Apps feature backend moves from Kubernetes to Docker to streamline App deployment and management". The first stable release was 2024-10-29. Automatic migration of k3s apps had a deadline of 2025-06-01, and third-party catalog apps (TrueCharts) needed manual redeployment.
- **N4. iXsystems announcement, "The Future of Electric Eel and Apps"** — https://forums.truenas.com/t/the-future-of-electric-eel-and-apps/5409 (opened; iXsystems community team, 2024-05-29). Reasons given: "Helm charts are powerful, but they are more complex and less portable"; "Docker is the industry standard"; users found K3s/Helm too complex; Docker/Compose eases import/export. **iX did not cite RAM or overhead.** The reasons were app-ecosystem portability and user complexity. That is the axis the claim does not measure. Staff follow-up: https://forums.truenas.com/t/apps-update-2024-06-06/6041 (opened; 2024-06-06, "we've begun the work to replace the k3s backend with Docker / Compose").
- **N5. rancher/k3os (archived)** — https://github.com/rancher/k3os (opened). "k3os is no longer maintained and has been superceeded by Elemental". The repository was archived 2023-12-08. The vendor behind k3s abandoned its own minimal k3s appliance OS. Other k3s-based appliance OS efforts have a lifecycle risk, though k3s itself remains active.
- **N6. Post-mortem: corrupted k3s SQLite on a Raspberry Pi 4 cluster** — https://mnguyen.fr/blog/2024-12-18-k3s-crash-postmortem (opened; SECONDARY, a personal blog). state.db reported "database disk image is malformed" after the problem built up quietly for about two days ("almost invisible and was 'accumulating' over time"). `.recover`/`.dump` failed, so the author had to restore a backup. The root cause was uncertain.
- **N7. CVE-2026-54250 (GHSA-jxr7-mqhw-9p98)** — https://www.suse.com/security/cve/CVE-2026-54250.html (opened). A path traversal in k3s etcd snapshot decompression, CVSS 5.8, fixed in 1.35.3/1.34.6/1.33.10. It is minor by itself. It is evidence for the patch treadmill: every OneMind 4 GB box carries the control-plane CVE surface that a Podman/Compose box does not.

## Strongest counter-argument

1. **The 4 GB RAM threshold sets the wrong boundary.** The lab answers the RAM question (2.5 GiB is left on 4 GiB). The documented way k3s fails on small boxes is **storage**: the cited k3s doc, N1, N2, N6, and Olares' SSD-only requirement. A Pi 5 4 GB on SD or eMMC passes the claim's gate and is the setup upstream warns against. A 2.7 GB/day write rate measured on virtio does not show the write amplification of a consumer SD card. The rule should be "≥4 GB **and** SSD/NVMe (or industrial pSLC eMMC with a measured endurance budget)". Without that, the claim promises a k3s platform on hardware where k3s is not supported upstream.
2. **A constraint-matched competitor chose k3s and turned back.** The claim's competitor framing ("only Olares is Kubernetes, at 8 GB") reads as "nobody has done k3s on small boxes because of RAM". TrueNAS SCALE did run a one-click app catalog on k3s on single appliances for several releases, then removed it (N3, N4). It did so for **app portability and user complexity**, not RAM. That is the same F-14 / M-6 axis OneMind's Market depends on: every self-hosted app ships a compose file, and few ship a Helm chart. The lab measures what iX did not care about and skips what made iX leave.
3. **The single-node SQLite fleet failure mode falls on a solo founder.** N2 is the deployment shape OneMind plans: hundreds of single-node SQLite k3s installs at customer sites. State.db bloat came back within weeks, the root cause was unknown, and the fix was manual SQL run with k3s stopped. A 40-minute lab run (30 MB db) cannot surface a failure that builds up over weeks (N6: "accumulating over time"). Podman/Compose boxes have no datastore that can fail this way.
4. **The measurement is too thin for a P0.** One run; writes taken from a 10-minute, 18 MiB delta, which may miss periodic kine compaction, image GC and log rotation; the "slim" profile exceeded the 5 GB/day threshold and was not re-run; the app is two idle containers; the OneMind core is not included. The 0.95 GiB delta is the floor. The cost at real load is unmeasured. CPU on a real Pi is about 30% of a core (resource-profiling page, Pi 4B), and the VM's 2.3% does not carry over.
5. **Lifecycle precedent.** k3OS (N5) shows that the vendor behind k3s cut its own k3s appliance OS. k3s continues, but its minor-version treadmill and CVE stream (N7) apply to every 4 GB box for 10 years. A Compose/Quadlet Lite tier plus NATS leaf would carry only the container runtime.

**Why this is "weakened", not "refuted":** the counter-examples (TrueNAS, Umbrel, HAOS) are single-box products. OneMind's requirements (M-4 scale to enterprise, UX-6 many machines in one view, F-14 one OS, the founder's DOKS + Argo reference) are multi-node. I found no source showing k3s failing on a ≥4 GB node **with SSD storage**. And N4's portability concern can be handled by a Market compiler that emits both formats. The claim's core design (k3s on platform nodes, NATS leaf on small devices) survives once the storage gate and the TrueNAS precedent are written in.

## Required corrections to the decision text

- Change the gate to: **"≥4 GB RAM and SSD/NVMe datastore (or industrial eMMC with a measured TBW budget); otherwise the node is a NATS leaf (optionally Podman Quadlet)."** Cite `requirements.md#L139-L141` and N1.
- Replace "the only Kubernetes-based competitor (Olares)" with: "Olares (k3s, 8 GB + SSD) is the only current k3s consumer OS. TrueNAS SCALE ran k3s apps and moved to Docker in 24.10 for portability and complexity reasons (N3/N4). k3OS was archived (N5)."
- Label the lab result as **one run, 10-minute write window, toy app, virtio disk**. Re-run state E to steady state, and add a 24-hour run with the OneMind core (NATS, auth, dashboard) before calling the 4 GB tier settled.
- Add a fleet-operations requirement: SQLite state.db size/integrity monitoring, automatic logical backups (`.backup`), and a remote compaction runbook (N2, N6).

## What evidence would flip the decision

- **To refuted (Lite/4 GB tier moves off k3s):** (a) the founder-hardware Pi 5 4 GB test (22-…) on **NVMe** leaves < 2.0 GiB available with the real OneMind core, or writes > 5 GB/day at steady state over 24 hours; (b) a 30-day soak on a 4 GB node shows state.db growth or compaction failures like N2; (c) the Market's app catalog cannot be expressed for both k3s and Compose by one compiler, so the TrueNAS portability problem lands on OneMind; (d) the target BOM for the Edge/Lite box is SD/eMMC-only (no NVMe), which puts the tier outside k3s's documented support.
- **To upheld:** (a) a 24-hour + 30-day Pi 5 4 GB NVMe run with the OneMind core that stays ≥ 2 GiB available and ≤ 5 GB/day, with state.db stable; (b) the decision text adopts the storage gate; (c) evidence that TrueNAS's reasons (Helm complexity/portability) are neutralized by OneMind's own `onemind.app.yaml` compiler (for example a prototype rendering 20 popular compose apps to k3s manifests without manual edits).
