## 1. Re-check of the cited sources
- The technical premise holds. The sysext man page and Lab W19-1 (C-W19-001 to 009) support E3 and E4. HA host networking (C-W8-026) and ROS 2 shared-memory DDS (C-W7-033) support E2. The competitor manifests (C-TD-home-assistant-os-026, C-TD-umbrelos-026, C-TD-olares-028) show that an app manifest plus a controller is standard.
- None of the cited sources shows that *all nine* contracts must be **frozen in release 1**. They show that each mechanism exists somewhere. The "fork" question is answered by E3 and E4 (and the image reservations in C-W19-049). E1, E2 and E5 to E9 live in k3s, the dashboard and the signing chain, which are all replaceable by an ordinary image update. They do not decide whether the OS needs a fork. They decide how big release 1 is.

## 2. Strongest counter-argument
**The conclusion merges two questions: "what must the image reserve so no fork is ever needed?" and "what ecosystem must ship on day one?". The second answer costs the most and is not supported.**

1. **Freezing nine public contracts before any third-party developer exists costs the most to undo.** A schemaVersion field exists so that a contract can evolve. Freezing E5 (MCP Apps UI), E6 (capability registry and gateway) and E8 (profiles) before real plugins exercise them risks freezing the wrong shape, and any later change breaks every plugin that adopted it.
2. **E1 picks Helm, and a direct comparable abandoned Helm for exactly this market.** iXsystems removed k3s and Helm from TrueNAS Apps in 24.10 because "K3s and Helm charts have been too complex and less reliable" and "more complex and less portable", citing user demand for Docker Compose [C-REF-W19-cost-001]. The third-party TrueCharts Helm catalogue lost support. That was the ecosystem cost of a contract change. Umbrel uses Compose and HAOS uses its own manifest. A home or prosumer appliance shipping a Helm-shaped public app contract is betting against the closest precedent. This does not make k3s wrong for OneMind's robot and fleet tier. It does make "Helm is the public plugin contract in release 1" a business risk that the conclusion does not name.
3. **E9 in release 1 implies a running review and countersigning operation from day one** (see W19-E9-trust-root-cost.md). For a solo developer that is a recurring staff cost before there is any revenue from a marketplace.

## 4. Verdict
**Weakened.** "No fork is needed" is upheld for E3 and E4 plus the image reservations. "Nine extension points must ship (frozen) in release 1" is not shown, and it is the expensive part. Recommended restatement: *release 1 must reserve E3, E4 and E9's root of trust in the image and must version every other contract. E1, E2 and E7 ship first-party only. E5, E6 and E8 are published as v0 or experimental until N external plugins exist.*

## 5. What would flip it
- **To upheld:** evidence that a comparable appliance (HAOS, Umbrel, Olares, StartOS) lost a material share of its ecosystem because a contract it shipped *later* could not be added without breaking apps. That would show late addition costs more than early freezing.
- **To refuted:** a costed build plan showing that E1 to E9 do not fit in release-1 scope for the team size, or a decision to use Compose rather than Helm for the public app contract. Either one makes the "release 1" condition unachievable as worded.
